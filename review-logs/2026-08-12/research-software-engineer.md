# Review: Research software engineer evaluating adoption

**Reviewed:** 2026-08-12 · **Scope:** landing page; `/book` and its TOC; `/book/organization`, `/book/how-we-work`, `/book/decisions`, `/book/licensing`, `/book/faq`, `/book/research-software`; `/people.html`, `/funding.html`, `/dashboard.html`, `/presentations.html` (status checks); `https://github.com/gaia-hazlab` org profile and 24 of its 26 public repositories, inspected for licence, releases, tags, CI runs, contributors, language mix, tests, `CONTRIBUTING`, `CITATION.cff`, and lockfiles · **Time spent:** 45 minutes (budget spent; I stopped rather than continued)

*I am a simulated reviewer, not a real one. This persona was constructed from the project's funding context and from what an RSE in a geoscience group typically needs. Every finding below is a hypothesis about a real reader, not evidence about one.*

## In one paragraph

My sixty-second sentence, written before I read anything else and not revised: *"GAIA HazLab builds digital twins of the Earth that fuse open multimodal sensor data with AI and cloud computing to forecast weather-compounded geohazards — landslides, liquefaction, floods — and offers a five-part software stack (DataHub, ModelHub, HazEvalHub, GaiaAgent, research software) I might be able to depend on."* That last clause is where I came unstuck. I went straight to the repositories, as I always do, and found something I did not expect: a project whose *written* engineering culture is better than most academic groups I have adopted from — a real `CONTRIBUTING.md` that says "we treat a missing licence as a bug", a licensing chapter that correctly warns against mixing copyleft into permissive, `pixi.lock` files in a dozen repositories, a Dockerfile pinned to `ghcr.io/prefix-dev/pixi:0.41.4` that runs `pixi install --locked` — sitting on top of an organisation where none of it is yet enforced. Eight public repositories carry no LICENSE file at all, including the two I would actually have adopted. There are no releases: one release and ten tags across twenty-six public repositories, so adoption means pinning commit hashes forever. Seventeen of the twenty-four repositories I sampled have one effective human committer. The taxonomy that is supposed to tell me what is safe to build on says of itself "Proposed — not yet ratified", and every one of the four decisions of record is still in status `proposed`. My decision: I will not adopt anything from this organisation today, and I would not get it past our legal review if I tried. What I *would* do is open one small pull request against `gaia-cli` — it is the only repository here shaped like a package I could depend on, it has tests, a passing CI job, a `pyproject.toml` with bounded version constraints and a named author — and see how it is handled. Ask me again in six months, after the licences land and the first `v0.1.0` tags exist; the distance between where this is and where it needs to be is measured in afternoons, not quarters.

## Findings

### 1. Eight public repositories have no LICENSE file, including both components I would have adopted — BLOCKER · D6
**Where:** https://github.com/gaia-hazlab/seis-hydro-2-sed · https://github.com/gaia-hazlab/gaia-data-downloaders · https://github.com/gaia-hazlab · https://gaia-hazlab.github.io
**Saw:** The About panel on `seis-hydro-2-sed` — the repository behind the flagship "WA-2025 river floods" demo on the landing page — shows no licence entry, and the repository tree contains no file matching `LICEN*`. The same is true of `gaia-data-downloaders`, `mt-rainier-smart-sensing`, `shred-landlab-prototypes`, `da-seis-groundfailure`, `landlab-debrisflow`, `gaia-translate-QA` and `awesome-gaia`. Meanwhile the footer of every page on the site reads: "© 2026 GAIA HazLab · University of Washington · Licensed under the MIT License." And https://gaia-hazlab.github.io/book/licensing states the default plainly: "Licence: MIT". *Observation.* The inference I draw — that nobody currently owns the job of checking this at repository-creation time — is an inference, and I mark it as one.
**Why it matters to me:** This is my hard stop, and it is not negotiable by me. I cannot get institutional legal approval to depend on code with no licence, whatever a footer or a book chapter says; "MIT by default" is not a grant of rights, a LICENSE file is. The cruellest part is that `gaia-data-downloaders` is the repository closest to the data layer I came here to avoid building, and it has eight distinct contributors — which is exactly the case where retroactive relicensing is hardest, because you have to go back and ask all eight. Every month this stays open makes it more expensive to fix. Your own `CONTRIBUTING.md` already says the right thing: "If a repository has none, say so in an issue — we treat a missing licence as a bug." I am saying so.
**Suggested fix:** Add MIT `LICENSE` files to the eight repositories this week — with eight contributors, `gaia-data-downloaders` first. Then add an org-level CI check, or a line in the repository-creation checklist, that fails when `LICENSE` is absent. There is already an open pull request titled "Create LICENSE" on `da-seis-groundfailure`, opened 2026-08-10; merge it.
**Confidence:** high

### 2. No releases and no versions anywhere — adoption means pinning commit hashes forever — MAJOR · D5, D7
**Where:** https://github.com/gaia-hazlab/gaia-cli/releases · https://github.com/gaia-hazlab/catalog/releases · https://github.com/gaia-hazlab
**Saw:** Across the 24 public repositories I checked, there is **one** published release in total (`gaia-data-downloaders`) and ten git tags in total, nine of which are in a single repository (`seis-hydro-2-sed`). `gaia-cli` — the most package-shaped thing here, with a console entry point `gaia = "gaia_cli.cli:app"` — has zero releases and zero tags, though its `pyproject.toml` declares `version = "0.1.0"`. Nothing in the organisation is on PyPI or conda-forge that I could find.
**Why it matters to me:** Without a tag I have nothing to pin to except a commit SHA, which means my lockfile records a point in your history rather than a contract with me. It also means there is no moment at which you have said "this interface is now something you may rely on" — semantic versioning is not bookkeeping, it is the promise that makes adoption cheaper than rebuilding. Five-year cost of adopting an untagged dependency is dominated by the unannounced breaking change I discover in a failing CI run at 6pm.
**Suggested fix:** Tag `gaia-cli` and `catalog` `v0.1.0` today — the version string is already in the `pyproject.toml`, so this is one `git tag` and one GitHub release each. Add a one-line release note convention. Do not wait for the code to feel finished; `0.x` explicitly means it is not.
**Confidence:** high

### 3. The "Research Software" chapter lists other people's packages, not yours — MAJOR · D5, D1
**Where:** https://gaia-hazlab.github.io/book/research-software/
**Saw:** The chapter I navigated to specifically to find out what GAIA maintains is a catalogue of the wider ecosystem: "ObsPy — The seismological observatory in Python", "xarray — N-dimensional labeled arrays and datasets in Python", "PyTorch — Deep learning framework", "Matplotlib — General-purpose plotting library", "Docker & Singularity". The only entries with any GAIA connection are NoisePy ("Developed by Marine Denolle and Yiyu Ni") and rslearn ("Collaborators include Akash Kharita and Scott Henderson"), both of which live in other organisations. There is no install command for anything GAIA itself publishes. Separately, `https://gaia-hazlab.github.io/book/chapters/research-software` returns HTTP 404 — the working path is `/book/research-software/`.
**Why it matters to me:** The landing page promises "One stack from sensor to forecast" with five named components — DataHub, ModelHub, HazEvalHub, GaiaAgent, Research Software — and I could not map a single one of those names onto a repository. I know what matplotlib is. What I needed from this page was three lines: here is the package, here is `pip install` or `pixi add`, here is who maintains it. A page that tells an RSE about PyTorch is telling him about the one thing he already has.
**Suggested fix:** Split the chapter. Keep the ecosystem list, retitle it "Software we build on", and put above it a short table of the software GAIA *publishes* — repository, one-line purpose, install command, maturity topic, named maintainer — even if that table currently has four rows and three of them say "no release yet". Four honest rows beat forty accurate ones about somebody else.
**Confidence:** high

### 5. The data layer I came for is 100 MB of notebooks with outputs committed — MAJOR · D5
**Where:** https://github.com/gaia-hazlab/gaia-data-downloaders · https://github.com/gaia-hazlab/shred-landlab-prototypes
**Saw:** GitHub's language breakdown for `gaia-data-downloaders` — described as "scripts to download weather data" — is Jupyter Notebook 103,749,532 bytes and HTML 8,707 bytes. There is no `.py` module and no test directory. `shred-landlab-prototypes` ("Prototype applications and pipelines combining PySHRED and Landlab") is 6,560,627 bytes of Jupyter Notebook and nothing else. `seis-hydro-2-sed` is 2.5 MB of notebook against 0.5 MB of Python. One of my four stop conditions is "every repository is a notebook", and to be fair that is *not* true here — `gaia-cli` (33 KB Python, 2 test paths), `gaia-literature-kb` (86 KB Python, 16 test paths), `landlab-debrisflow` (341 KB Python) and `gwl-space-time-smooth` (18 test paths, a passing `Tests` workflow) are real code. But the specific thing I wanted to adopt is not.
**Why it matters to me:** A hundred megabytes in a language breakdown means the notebooks were committed with their outputs in them, so every clone drags the full history and every diff is unreadable. Notebooks are results; I cannot import one, cannot unit-test one, and cannot review a pull request against one. If the data-download layer is where GAIA's real value to me lives, it needs to be a package with functions in it and the notebooks demoting themselves to examples.
**Suggested fix:** Extract the download logic from `gaia-data-downloaders` into a small module and keep the notebooks as thin callers — or, better, fold the capability into `gaia-cli`, which already has the right shape, tests and CI. Add `nbstripout` as a pre-commit hook so outputs stop entering the history.
**Confidence:** high

### 6. Everything that tells me what is safe to depend on is still in status "proposed" — MAJOR · D6
**Where:** https://gaia-hazlab.github.io/book/organization/ · https://gaia-hazlab.github.io/book/decisions
**Saw:** The organization chapter labels itself "Proposed — not yet ratified", and states: "This taxonomy is a proposal awaiting a numbered decision" and "Until it is ratified, repository topics may not match what is described here." The decisions register carries four entries — 001 funding acknowledgment, 002 system of record, 003 meeting schedule, 004 Google identity — all with status `proposed` and all with an em-dash where the date should be, on a page that states: "Nothing counts as decided until it has a number here. If it lives only in a Doc, a Slack thread, or someone's memory of a call, it is still a proposal." Operationally, only three repositories carry `gaia-stable` — `catalog`, `awesome-gaia` and `gaia-hazlab.github.io` — meaning that of everything in this organisation that is *software I could import*, exactly one is tagged stable and the list of links is tagged the same.
**Why it matters to me:** The maturity taxonomy is the single most useful thing on this site for my decision — `gaia-incubating` telling me "the interface may change without warning" is precisely the signal I want, and I would point other projects at the three-axis topic scheme as a model. **Confidence:** high

## What worked

**The environment story is genuinely better than most projects I evaluate.** `pixi.lock` is present in eleven of the repositories I checked — `gaia-cli`, `catalog`, `skypilot-hyak`, `gaia-data-downloaders`, `gaia-agentic-ai`, `gwl-space-time-smooth`, `mt-rainier-smart-sensing`, `solus-stac`, `gaia-literature-kb`, `seis-hydro-2-sed`, `gaia-hazlab.github.io` — and `gaia-cli`'s `pyproject.toml` uses bounded constraints (`"cyclopts>=4.20.0,<5"`, `"xvec>=0.5.2,<0.6"`) rather than the unpinned `environment.yml` I usually find. The `seis-hydro-2-sed` Dockerfile pinning to `ghcr.io/prefix-dev/pixi:0.41.4` and running `pixi install --locked`, with a comment explaining that this makes the container match the dev environment "byte-for-byte", is the single best engineering artefact on this visit. Protect this; it is the part of the project that is already ahead of its peers.

**CI exists and passes, and the failures are visible.** `gwl-space-time-smooth`'s `Tests` workflow ran green on `main` on 2026-07-22 and on feature branches before merge; `gaia-cli`'s `Test` workflow shows a legible pattern of red on branch, green on `main` (failure on `add-opentopo-dem` 2026-07-07, success on `main` the same day). Dependabot is enabled on `gaia-cli` and `gaia-agentic-ai`. A red badge on a branch is more informative to me than a green one in a README, and I could see both.

**The org `CONTRIBUTING.md` treats an outside contributor as an adult.** "Pull requests are welcome on every public repository"; "To get a tool listed, use the *List my tool* issue template — that is level 0 and it needs nobody's permission"; "The person who wrote the code usually merges it"; "If your contribution is substantial, add yourself to the repository's `CITATION.cff`. Software citation is not a courtesy here." The five-level relationship ladder on the organization chapter — Listed, Containerised, Demonstrated, Benchmarked, Adopted, each "a larger commitment by us than the one before, so each is agreed rather than assumed" — is the clearest statement of *what adopting your stuff obliges you to* that I have read from an academic group. Ratify it.

**The three-axis topic scheme makes 26 repositories browsable without a hand-maintained index.** Umbrella / category / relationship / maturity, one value each, with the `github.com/search?q=org%3Agaia-hazlab+topic%3Agaia-data` browse links on the profile README. I found every repository I wanted in under two minutes. This is a pattern I would copy.

## What I could not judge

- **Whether the science is right.** Whether soil-moisture memory carries predictive skill for slope failure, whether the seismic-to-bedload inversion is sound — outside my competence entirely. I evaluated software, not results.
- **Container images in a registry.** `gh api orgs/gaia-hazlab/packages` returned HTTP 403 ("You need at least `read:packages` scope"), so I cannot state that no images exist — only that no repository carries the `gaia-container` topic, no workflow builds an image, and no registry is linked from the site. If images do exist, a reader like me has no path to them.
- **Private repositories.** Seven of the 33 repositories in the organisation are private (`thunderquakes`, `landslide-digital-twin`, `fire-debrisflow-ml`, `landslide-data-prep`, `gaia-nhsm-deagg`, `gaia-slack-digest`, `gaia-dot-github`). Work may be much further along inside them; I cannot see it, and neither can any adopter.
- **Actual pull-request turnaround.** My persona's decisive test — open one small PR and watch how it is handled — takes weeks, not the 45 minutes I had. The open-PR ages I found are a proxy, not the measurement.
- **Design and accessibility beyond the obvious.** Contrast ratios, keyboard reach and alt-text coverage are not my field; finding 11 is the only one I felt qualified to raise, and only because it showed up in a plain HTTP fetch.
- **Whether `gwl-space-time-smooth`'s 134 open issues are neglect or an intentional generated work queue.** From outside, these look identical.

## My signature question

*What is the maintenance commitment, stated by a named person, for each thing I would depend on?*

**There is none, for anything, anywhere on this site.** The roster at `data/team.json` behind https://gaia-hazlab.github.io/people.html names 24 people, and every role is an academic rank or funding position — "Lead PI", "Co-PI", "Senior Personnel", "Research Scientist", "Postdoctoral Researcher", "Graduate Student", "Undergraduate Student", "Collaborator". Not one person is identified as the maintainer of a named software component, and no page maps a repository to a human. `CITATION.cff` exists in exactly one of the 12 repositories I checked for it (`seis-hydro-2-sed`); the other eleven, including `gaia-cli` and `catalog`, have none. The only maintainer commitment stated anywhere is conditional and forward-looking — Level 4 "requires a named maintainer and a numbered decision" on https://gaia-hazlab.github.io/book/organization/ — and since nothing has reached Level 4 and no decision has left `proposed`, no name has yet been attached to anything.

Flagged, per my own rule — every component I would depend on, with no named maintainer: **`gaia-cli`** (the data-staging CLI; `pyproject.toml` lists Scott Henderson as *author*, which is provenance, not a commitment), **`catalog`** (the data catalogue and web map, tagged `gaia-stable` with three contributors), **`gaia-data-downloaders`** (the download layer; eight contributors, no licence, no maintainer), **the four STAC catalogues** (`solus-stac`, `prism-stac`, `precip-stac`, `landlab-stac`, all single-contributor), and **the container base image**, which does not exist yet and therefore has no one to abandon it. Concretely: adding a `CITATION.cff` with a maintainer field, plus one line in each README saying who answers issues and roughly how fast, would move D6 and D7 more than any amount of further prose — and it is an afternoon's work.
